Our model represents a complex end to end mailing system shipping commercial and personal mail from Lagos, Nigeria to London, England. Our process begins in a post office in Lagos,  goes through the transportation and customs of an airport that brings it onto a plane, is placed on a cargo flight to London, where it leaves the post office in Lagos’ scope of responsibility. Built on AnyLogic, our model represents all the complex steps, delays, and bottlenecks that a real end to end supply chain of this nature would naturally demonstrate.
Beginning from the post office, we placed a batch with a size of 10 after our start to represent the loading of a mail truck to transport the goods from the post office to the airport. This batch was followed by a delay & queue to represent the time it would take to load up the truck followed by the traffic that goes directly to the airport. We have certain parameters on both the queue and delay that represent how long it would take to load 10 batches and about 30 minutes to represent traffic towards the direction of the Lagos airport. Once arrived at the airport, there is an unbatch placed to show how long it would take to unload our materials, followed by a queue and delay pairing that shows the time it would take to get through customs and the foot traffic that is required to transport a load of mail through an airport. After this, there is another batch to show the loading of the airplane, followed by a queue that is much longer than our prior queues to represent the 6 hour flight from Lagos to London. 
At this step, the team tried to make our model more complex. But ran into troubles with our queue that represented our flight, and the baggage check/customs delay. We had changed every parameter that could give us a difference in results when running our AnyLogic model and the entire model began to not run. Once we tweaked the flight queue to more accurately fit the parameters that demonstrated a realistic flight and removed the queue for customs, the model ran flawlessly. But, we still needed to find a way to represent the time delay for how long it takes to get the mail through customs. This is when we decided to recreate the model in a separate file to see if the file name, that seemed corrupted and linked to another one of our files which was causing complications when designating an agent to our delays, and try to run it all again. We added a resource pool with a capacity of 50, matching our mails capacity, slowing down the transport time by loading batches individually prior to loading them in bulk. We also got rid of the delay for customs after finding out that no matter how we changed the parameters, that it wasn't going to be able to run with this model. But, at this point we were still looking for alternative routes to represent the time that it would take to get the mail through the airport's baggage check. 
After some final alterations of the constraints and time periods aligned with other queue and delay points we were able to have the ALP file run successfully and not have the queue interrupt the process. The rest of the model represents the flight, and a second customs check queue+delay handled by a hypothetical quality control team for international cargo shipments coming out of the Lagos airport. The model has the capability to run smoothly at multiple capacities and with multiple different time constraints placed on the delays segments, minus the delay and queue representing the flights because that will most likely stay consistent, and accurately represents the main supply chain nodes for a network that represents the complexity of  an air cargo shipment from Lagos to London.
